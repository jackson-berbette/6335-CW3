**Purpose:** Reproduce the historical inventory, warehouse operations diagnostic, and baseline performance analyses using the supplied case data.

## 5. Data and shared conventions

The analyses use 11 supplied CSVs. Their roles are:

| File | Main use |
| --- | --- |
| `clients.csv`, `client_sites.csv` | Client and delivery-location characteristics |
| `skus.csv`, `suppliers.csv` | SKU attributes, pallet dimensions, suppliers |
| `slots.csv`, `docks.csv` | Rack geometry, opening heights, travel and lift distances |
| `opening_stock.csv`, `current_stock.csv` | Opening/closing pallet identities and storage positions |
| `inbound.csv`, `outbound.csv` | Historical pallet receipts and shipments |
| `trucks.csv` | Shipping truck assignments, loads and times |

Historical observation window: **July 7, 2025–July 5, 2026** (364 days; 52 complete weeks). Daytime outbound movements occur before nighttime inbound movements on the same date. One pallet occupies at most one slot at a time. The assignment's FIFO and height-fit rules are evaluated against recorded pallet identities and dates.

**SKU velocity:** Rank SKUs by annual outbound pallet volume; assign class A to approximately the first 80% of volume, B to the next 15%, and C to the remainder, using cumulative share *before* each SKU row and SKU ID for tie-breaking. These 80%/95% boundaries are an **analytical choice**, not professor-mandated cutoffs. Reuse Task 1's exact classification in Tasks 2 and 3.

**Coverage:**

- **1(a):** Clients, delivery locations and geographic/shipping profile.
- **1(b):** SKU mix, pallet characteristics, annual volume, Pareto velocity classes.
- **1(c):** Outbound demand patterns, departure times, weekly trends and variability.
- **1(d):** Truck loading, capacity utilization and delivery-pattern diagnostics.
- **1(e):** Inbound receipts, suppliers, purchase orders and night workload.
- **1(f):** Daily SKU inventory reconstruction, average/peak stock and stock coverage.

**Principal generated files:**

- `outputs_task1/TASK1_REQUIRED_sku_velocity_stock.csv` — shared SKU classification and stock measures.
- `outputs_task1/TASK1_summary_metrics.csv` — consolidated results.

- `outputs_task1/validation.txt` — implemented consistency checks.
- Additional `1a_` through `1f_` CSVs and figures.

**Method notes:** Daily ending inventory equals opening stock plus receipts minus shipments; stock must not be negative before the nighttime receipt. Average and peak inventory are computed over daily end-of-day balances. A 26-pallet truck capacity is used in the truck diagnostics. Statistical descriptions do not by themselves establish the cause of partial truckloads or changes in demand.

## 7. Task 2 — Existing warehouse operations

**2(a) Layout:** Count aisles, bays, levels and slots; analyze rack opening heights and tall-slot locations; verify computed travel distances against the coordinates and inspect the generated visualization against the supplied facility map.

**2(b) Putaway:** Compare observed assignments by SKU velocity, client, rack level and travel distance; measure excess opening height and daily occupancy-weighted unused height. The enhanced feasible-slot analysis checks approximately **every tenth putaway** against available compatible slots from the **actual receiving door**. This is a sampled, descriptive comparison; same-timestamp pallet order is approximated using pallet ID and does not prove driver intent.

**2(c) FIFO:** Audit both (i) exact chronological pick order and (ii) whether the correct oldest pallet *set* ships within each day. These are distinct standards. Analyze storage durations of shipped pallets and end-of-year ages of pallets still in stock; the latter are right-censored observations, not completed storage durations.

**2(d) Forklift workload:** Compute slot-movement time using the case formula:

```text
slot_move_minutes = 2 * travel_ft / 264 + 2 * lift_ft / 60 + 1.5
```

Report the separately specified **1.8 minutes per truck/staging-lane dock movement**. Distinguish the assignment's prescribed slot-movement labor hours from total handling work including dock moves. Produce daily and weekly day/night workload tables and identify peaks.

**Principal generated files:** Layout CSVs/maps (`2a_`), putaway and height-efficiency diagnostics (`2b_`), chronological and daily-batch FIFO audits plus aging tables (`2c_`), daily/weekly forklift workloads and a **blank** Digital Twin experiment worksheet (`2d_`). Exact filenames are in `outputs_task2/`.

**Not automated:** Task 2(d) requires **actual web-based Digital Twin experiments** on the same historical day with varying forklift counts. Record truck waiting/delays, whether nighttime work ends by midnight, and the point of diminishing returns. The script does not run the Digital Twin or fabricate simulation outcomes. The written Task 2 report must also identify three questionable existing practices with evidence.

## 8. Task 3 — Baseline scorecard and improvement analysis

**3(a):** Define performance measures, formulas, data sources, design sensitivity and stakeholder relevance (StackBay, clients, forklift drivers).

**3(b):** Calculate the historical baseline: travel, slot-movement labor, occupancy, inventory, space fit, and related normalized measures. Validate historical pallet identity and slot occupancy where the recorded data permits.

**3(c):** Break down travel and labor by SKU velocity, client, rack level and aisle. Define the fastest 5% as the **60 SKUs with highest annual outbound pallet volume** (ceil(5% × 1,189)), with SKU ID as the tie-breaker. Specify whether a reported travel share covers picking only or combined inbound and outbound slot movements; the enhanced analysis reports the latter.

**3(d):** Compare current placement with two clearly distinguished calculations:

1. **Relaxed opportunity bound:** Each movement independently chooses a nearby height-compatible slot, without enforcing occupancy. This is an optimistic bound, **not an implementable design**.
2. **Capacity-aware greedy scenario:** Keep opening stock in its recorded slots; replay historical shipments before nighttime receipts; assign incoming top-60 SKU pallets to nearby currently empty height-compatible slots, using the actual receiving door. Other incoming pallets retain their historical slot if available or use a feasible alternative. Recalculate all movements using the actual shipping/receiving doors. Enforce unique occupied slots, fit and inventory reconciliation.

The greedy scenario is **feasible with respect to modeled slot availability and pallet fit**, but is not a globally optimized or operationally validated warehouse design. It does not simulate forklift congestion, truck departure performance, or traffic conflicts. Do not describe its modeled savings as guaranteed.

**3(e):** Interpret which baseline inefficiencies arise from warehouse geometry, demand and pallet constraints versus controllable storage decisions. The generated evidence summary is a starting point; the team must write and substantiate its final judgment.

**Principal generated files:**

- `outputs_task3/3a_metric_definitions.csv`

- `outputs_task3/3b_baseline_scorecard.csv`

## 9. Validation and limitations

The scripts include checks for key data-integrity and physical-consistency conditions, including known IDs, pallet identity, inventory reconciliation, rack-height fit and occupied-slot uniqueness where applicable. A successful run demonstrates that **implemented checks** passed; it does not independently verify every source timestamp, causal interpretation or real-world operating assumption.

In particular:

- **Task 1:** ABC thresholds are a documented design choice; trends and truck-loading explanations are descriptive.
- **Task 2:** Feasible-slot sampling and tied timestamp order are approximations; daily-batch FIFO is not identical to chronological pick-order FIFO; the facility map needs visual review; Digital Twin runs are outstanding until manually performed.
- **Task 3:** The relaxed bound ignores capacity; the capacity-aware scenario is a greedy heuristic, not a guaranteed improvement or queueing simulation.
